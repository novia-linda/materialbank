Theme C – Group Case: Who should receive the Nordic Freight bonus?

Eyebrow: AI for Business · Group Case · Theme C

Time budget: approx. 90 minutes

Submission: One shared decision brief for the group – PowerPoint or Word. Use one page/slide per candidate plus one final recommendation page/slide.

Nordic Freight has one quarterly performance bonus available. Four employees in Sales & Customer Service are eligible. The bonus cannot be split. Your group must recommend one employee and be ready to defend the decision with evidence.

Viktigt: There is no fixed bonus formula and no single KPI decides the answer. Different data sources show different parts of employee performance. Your task is to combine them responsibly.

Sektion: START TOGETHER

Sektionstext: Understand the decision, divide the evidence sources and agree on the final output.

Understand the case

Nordic Freight is a fictional logistics company in the Vaasa region with about 23 employees. Four employees in Sales & Customer Service are being considered for one quarterly performance bonus:

  • Johan Andersson
  • Maja Nyman
  • Susan Granholm
  • Elias Smeds

Your group will investigate several independent evidence sources. Each detective first works separately. Then you must bring the evidence together and decide who should receive the bonus.

Important limitation

This is a fictional learning exercise. In a real organisation, a bonus decision should normally use broader HR information, clear criteria and an appropriate decision process. Here you are practising how to analyse limited evidence and make the uncertainty visible.

Sektion: PARALLEL DETECTIVE WORK

Sektionstext: Analyse one source deeply, but bring back evidence about all four candidates.

Prepare your detective evidence brief

You have about 20 minutes. Use Microsoft Copilot to help calculate, compare and summarise. Do not ask AI only “Who should get the bonus?” Your job is to understand your own evidence source first.

For each of the four employees, bring back:

  • 1–3 useful figures, patterns or recurring themes
  • one clear strength
  • one concern, limitation or counterevidence
  • one sentence explaining what the group should know before judging this employee

Starter prompt for any detective

I am one detective in a group exercise about a fictional logistics company called Nordic Freight. Four employees are being considered for one performance bonus: Johan Andersson, Maja Nyman, Susan Granholm and Elias Smeds. I have been given one evidence source. Help me analyse this source carefully. Do not choose the bonus winner for me. For each employee, identify useful figures or repeated themes, one clear strength, one concern or limitation, and anything that could make a simple comparison unfair. Show how you calculated any important figures.

[[END H4]]

Use the questions for your detective role

Business & Profitability detective

  1. Who has the highest total NetRevenueEUR?
  2. Who wins the most deals, and who has the strongest conversion rate?
  3. How do discounts, correction costs and credits/refunds change the picture?
  4. Calculate and compare contribution margin: NetRevenueEUR − DirectTransportCostEUR − CorrectionCostEUR − CreditOrRefundEUR. Also compare contribution margin as a percentage of net revenue.
  5. Does the employee with the highest revenue also create the strongest economic result?
  6. What is the strongest business argument for each candidate – and one caution?

[[END H4]]

Customer Experience detective

  1. Compare the number of survey responses for the four employees.
  2. Compare average satisfaction, helpfulness, clarity, recommendation and likelihood to use Nordic Freight again.
  3. What repeated positive themes appear in the comments for each employee?
  4. What repeated concerns or trade-offs appear?
  5. Do the comments tell you anything that the averages hide?
  6. What is the strongest customer-experience argument for each candidate – and one caution?

[[END H4]]

Driver Perspective detective

  1. Compare the driver ratings for information completeness, route realism, schedule realism, change handling and trust on complex jobs.
  2. Which themes are repeated by several drivers for each employee?
  3. Where do drivers describe avoidable extra work or uncertainty?
  4. Where do drivers describe planning that makes their work easier?
  5. Are there comments that contradict the main pattern?
  6. What is the strongest driver-perspective argument for each candidate – and one caution?

[[END H4]]

Operations & Case Handling detective

  1. Who handles the most cases, and who has the shortest average resolution time?
  2. Compare first-contact resolution, reopened cases, corrections and follow-up.
  3. Compare the share of Standard, Advanced and Critical cases for each employee.
  4. Who receives escalated cases or cases that have already had previous handlers?
  5. Does your interpretation change when you consider case complexity and case history?
  6. What is the strongest operations argument for each candidate – and one caution?

[[END H4]]

Internal Staff Perspective detective – group of five only

  1. What themes are repeated by different colleagues for each candidate?
  2. What do colleagues say about reliability, teamwork and problem solving?
  3. Which employee seems to create work for others, and which employee seems to save work for others?
  4. Which comments describe contributions that may not appear in individual KPI figures?
  5. Are there comments that challenge the dominant story?
  6. What is the strongest internal-staff argument for each candidate – and one caution?

[[END H4]]

Sektion: BRING THE EVIDENCE TOGETHER

Sektionstext: Build a complete picture of one employee at a time.

Create four candidate pages or slides

Create one shared PowerPoint or Word document. Use one page/slide for each candidate. Each detective contributes evidence from their own source.

Use this structure on every candidate page/slide:

PerspectiveWhat to include
BusinessKey commercial/profitability evidence
CustomersImportant ratings and comment themes
DriversWhat the people carrying out the transports notice
Operations & casesVolume, speed, quality, complexity and case context
Internal staffInclude only if your group has five members
Overall picture2–4 bullets: strongest reasons for and against giving this person the bonus

Viktigt: Do not paste large AI summaries into the shared document. Select only evidence that helps the group understand the candidate.

Look for agreement and conflict between sources

Before choosing the bonus recipient, discuss:

  1. Which findings appear in more than one independent source?
  2. Where do the sources point in different directions?
  3. Did any new source change your first impression of an employee?
  4. Are any comparisons unfair because employees handle different types of work?
  5. Which candidate has the strongest case if you value business results? Customer experience? Operational reliability? Difficult problem solving?

Sektion: MAKE THE BONUS DECISION

Sektionstext: Recommend one employee – and test your own reasoning before you finish.

Choose one bonus recipient

Nordic Freight can award one performance bonus. You cannot split it between the candidates.

Add one final page/slide with:

  • Our recommendation: one employee
  • Why: the 3–5 strongest pieces of evidence from different sources
  • Strongest counterargument: the best reason not to choose this person
  • Runner-up: include this if the decision was close, with one sentence explaining why the runner-up finished second
  • What we still do not know: one important limitation or additional piece of information management would ideally want

Do not solve the case with a simple average

There is no official weighting such as “40% sales + 30% customers + 30% operations”. Your group must decide what the evidence means and explain the trade-offs. A different group may make a different recommendation if its reasoning is well supported.

[[END H4]]

Challenge your recommendation

Before you finish, try to prove yourselves wrong.

  1. What is the strongest evidence against your chosen winner?
  2. What is the strongest case for another candidate?
  3. Are you overvaluing one data source because it contains easy-to-read numbers?
  4. Would your decision change if Nordic Freight's priority were different?

Change your decision if the evidence gives you a good reason to do so.

Sektion: SHOW AND DEFEND

Sektionstext: Show the candidate profiles and be ready for individual questions.

Prepare to show your result

Show the teacher your shared decision brief. You do not need a separate presentation.

Be ready to move through:

  1. Johan Andersson
  2. Maja Nyman
  3. Susan Granholm
  4. Elias Smeds
  5. Your final bonus recommendation

The teacher may ask any group member about any data source or candidate. Use the final minutes to make sure everyone understands the group's evidence and reasoning.

Final checklist

Checklista: Our bonus investigation is ready

  • Every detective analysed all four candidates.
  • Our shared document has one page/slide per candidate.
  • Each candidate page uses evidence from all available perspectives.
  • We distinguished observations from possible explanations.
  • We noticed important limitations or unfair comparisons.
  • We selected one bonus recipient.
  • Our recommendation uses evidence from several sources.
  • We identified the strongest counterargument.
  • We included a runner-up if the decision was close.
  • Everyone in the group can explain the final reasoning.
