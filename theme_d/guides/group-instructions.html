Theme D - Group Case: Build the Nordic Freight knowledge assistant

AI for Business | Group Case | Theme D

Time budget: Approximately 90 minutes of group work, followed by a short teacher check, one group at a time.

Submission: One internal Gem, checked knowledge documents, a saved test report and a JSON progress backup. Show these to the teacher. No whole-class presentation or slide deck is required.

Nordic Freight has a new summer trainee who answers customer telephone calls about domestic parcels and small goods within Finland. The company information is spread across a policy, a shipment register, emails, phone transcripts and a printed guide. Your group will make it usable in one knowledge assistant.

Important: This is an internal support tool, not a public customer chatbot. All business information is fictional. Work with personal, free Google accounts and only the supplied case data. Do not buy a subscription or use real customer information.

START TOGETHER

Understand the case and choose different knowledge responsibilities.

Understand the goal and agree on a working setup

Each person first becomes the expert on one original source. Later, bring your knowledge together, test the Gem and verify every answer in the originals. An AI answer is not a verified answer.

Use the fixed case snapshot: Friday 12 June 2026, 14:00, Finland local time. This is the meaning of today in shipment questions, even if you take the course later.

Choose four or five roles. Person 1 owns the Gem. Agree on one working Drive folder and one computer/browser for the shared Test Lab. The others keep their original documents open on their own computers. The Test Lab does not synchronise between computers.

Work in parallel: become the expert

Spend about 20 minutes on your own role. Do not all read the same document. Each person must bring a concrete result back to the group.

Person 1 | Company policy and Gem setup

Your source: Company Policy (PDF, P01-P07).

Read the policy. Create the group's Gem in your own free Google account and attach the policy. Make two or three small test questions of your own. Find each answer in the policy; note what worked and what still needs checking. You are the policy expert, not only the technical operator.

Initial instructions: use a short starting rule, not a finished solution.

You support a Nordic Freight summer trainee who answers customer calls. Answer using the attached approved company information. If the sources do not support an answer, say so. Do not invent company facts or claim that you performed a real action.

Bring back: the saved Gem, the most important boundaries, one policy example with its P-number and any setup issue. The final instructions will be a group decision.

Open the Google setup and fallback guide.

Person 2 | Shipment information

Your source: Shipment Register (Excel, 20 fictional shipments).

Read the Read me and Data dictionary sheets. Open a working copy in Google Sheets. Understand the columns, statuses, empty cells and the fixed snapshot date. Find one delivered shipment, one still moving and a customer with more than one shipment. Prepare two short test questions and check them manually.

Bring back: one clearly identified working Sheet, access for the Gem owner, an explanation of the fields and your test observations. Do not add a second active copy of the same data to the Gem.

The original 20 rows stay unchanged. Later you will add a new fictional shipment for an update experiment. If the Gem cannot attach/read the Sheet, use the matching static TXT file for knowledge, but verify answers in the original workbook.

Person 3 | Knowledge hidden in emails

Your source: Customer Email Archive (PDF, E01-E12) or editable Word version.

Read all 12 threads. Find repeated questions, including questions asked in different words. Select five useful topics and create an initial FAQ with five questions and answers. Keep important conditions. Include the original E-number or numbers in every entry. Note other topics that you did not include yet.

You may use a normal AI chat to help organise the material, but check each proposed entry yourself. Do not invent new rules or upload the raw archive straight into the group Gem.

Bring back: one checked FAQ document, five entries, a short list of omitted topics and the original archive for verification. You remain the group's email expert.

Person 4 | Knowledge hidden in calls

Your source: Phone Transcripts (PDF, C01-C10) or editable Word version.

Read the calls. Select five recurring or useful situations. For each, record the caller's question, the answer or next step, what information the trainee needs, and when a person takes over. Keep the original C-number or numbers. List topics or details left out of the first version.

You may ask AI for help, but verify its summary. A shorter version must not turn an uncertain request into an approved action. Keep the original transcripts outside the Gem for the evidence check.

Bring back: one checked phone-knowledge document with five entries, your omitted-topic notes and the original call evidence.

Person 5 | Packing desk knowledge (five-person groups only)

Your source: Packing Desk Guide (PDF, G01-G05).

This printed guide has been checked and is still current. Turn its five sections into five short knowledge entries. Use wording a caller might use, retain conditions and stop/ask rules, and keep the G-number in each entry. Do not add services or prices from the internet.

Bring back: one checked packing-knowledge document and a clear explanation of the boundary between giving information and approving a special arrangement. Four-person groups do not need this source.

Knowledge entry template and an AI starter prompt

Use the editable knowledge-entry template or its plain-text version. Copy the fields for each entry; you do not need an elaborate layout.

Help me turn this approved training source into five concise knowledge entries. Merge repeated questions, preserve conditions and handover rules, and include original email/call/guide IDs for every entry. Use only this source. Do not invent company facts. List topics or details left out of the five entries. I will check the entries myself.
BRING THE KNOWLEDGE TOGETHER

Combine different sources without losing their meaning or origin.

Share, check and attach the knowledge

Each person briefly explains what their source contains, what they prepared and any limitation. Check the new entries against the originals before adding them to the Gem. Keep the originals accessible: an entry must still lead back to its original evidence.

The saved Gem should contain the Company Policy, one shipment source, the checked email FAQ and the checked phone knowledge. Five-person groups also add checked packing knowledge. Do not attach both the original archive and its FAQ as a shortcut; the task is to improve the selected knowledge.

Five entries are a starting point, not a limit. If testing later reveals something you omitted, find the original passage and improve or add an entry. Do not change the original policy, archive or baseline shipment facts just to make a test pass.

Decide the final Gem instructions together

Separate behaviour from company facts. The instructions should explain how the Gem helps the trainee, which sources it may use, how to handle missing information, how to show evidence and when to hand over. Keep specific service facts in the knowledge documents.

Instruction structure to complete
ROLE AND PURPOSE
[Who uses the Gem, and what help do they need?]
APPROVED KNOWLEDGE
[Which sources may supply company facts? How should the snapshot and ShipmentIDs be used?]
RESPONSE PROCESS
[How should the Gem find evidence, ask necessary questions and explain the answer?]
BOUNDARIES AND HANDOVER
[Which facts or actions must not be invented? When does a person take over?]
OUTPUT
[Choose a short format useful during a telephone call, with a source pointer and next step when needed.]

Use AI to improve wording only after making your own decisions. Check that it has not added prices, services, rights, deadlines or technical abilities.

Save the Gem. The Google setup guide includes an attachment fallback and explains the difference between a Drive source and a static upload.

ASK, VERIFY, IMPROVE

Twenty mixed questions; every answer needs original evidence.

Open the Test Lab and verify all 20 questions

Open the Nordic Freight Knowledge Assistant Test Lab. Choose four or five people to load the right question bank. Use one shared browser session. A person can operate the computer, but the whole group takes part.

For every question: start a fresh Gem conversation, copy in the question, record a short answer summary in the lab, find the supporting passage in the original source and record the evidence ID and who checked it. Tick all sources actually used. Do not mark an answer correct only because it sounds reasonable.

Every core question has a supported answer somewhere in your provided original materials. Sometimes the answer is a recorded fact; sometimes it is a condition or an instruction to ask a person. Some answers need two sources. Your first five-entry summaries may not include every detail.

What counts as evidence?

Use an original reference such as Email E07, Call C03, Policy P03 or ShipmentID NF-1043 and its relevant columns. Explain briefly what the passage confirms. A Gem citation tells you where to look; it does not complete the check for you. For multi-source answers, record each original passage needed for the complete answer.

Repair problems and retest the same question

If an answer is wrong, partial or not found, return to the originals. Did the first summary omit a detail? Is a source missing? Does a heading need better wording? Does the answer need two sources? Was the wrong shipment or date used?

Record what you changed. Improve the reusable knowledge or instructions, then ask the same question in a fresh Gem conversation without including the correct answer in the prompt. Keep the first-test result and the retest result in the lab. Mark Verified only after a correct answer and an original-source check.

Do not assume every group will have the same failures. If everything works, verify it carefully; do not manufacture an error. If a technical block persists, save an honest incomplete report and speak to the teacher.

OBSERVE UPDATED INFORMATION

A separate experiment, not a requirement for perfect live technology.

Add a fictional shipment and observe the update

Open Update experiment in the Test Lab. Add one new fictional shipment with a unique reference, for example NF-1099, to the agreed working source. Leave the original 20 records unchanged, so the core questions and teacher answer key remain valid.

Record the new value and saved time. Try waiting about one minute, then ask about the new reference in a fresh Gem conversation. Do not include the answer in your prompt. Record the actual wait and what the Gem answered. Keep any manual re-upload, reattachment or text-copy step separate from automatic updating.

A failed or delayed update is an acceptable experimental observation. The aim is to explain what happened, not to repair Google. A successful read after manual file replacement is not proof of an automatic live connection. Stop after a short trial and report the result.

SHOW THE TEACHER

One group at a time, with evidence and individual explanations.

Save your work and prepare the teacher check

Your target is 20 source-verified questions and a recorded update observation. Generate the report, choose Print / Save as PDF, and download the JSON progress backup. The report is for reading; the JSON backup lets you restore the working session. Keep your knowledge documents and originals available.

One group at a time shows its work to the teacher. You do not present to the whole class. The teacher will choose some questions from your 20-question bank. Run them in the Gem and show the original evidence. Each group member must be able to explain their own source, contribution and a check or improvement. Person 2 also explains the update experiment, whether it worked or not.

No slide deck is needed. Do not spend your work time making a presentation. Keep the Gem open on the owner's computer and have the report ready.

Final checklist
  • Our 20 answers have been checked in the original sources, with exact references and a named checker.
  • Failed tests were improved and retested, or a technical block is honestly reported to the teacher.
  • Our FAQ and other knowledge documents retain their original evidence IDs.
  • We recorded what happened in the update experiment without claiming automatic updating after a manual step.
  • We saved a PDF report and JSON backup.
  • Every person can explain their own role in a short group meeting with the teacher.
